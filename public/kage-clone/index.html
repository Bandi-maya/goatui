01 — Thresholds

Charred cypress, worn stone, one gate left open.

Kage begins where the city stops: a mountain gate of cedar burned black, standing in its own weather. Climb the worn steps and the worship hall lifts out of the mist, its paper screens lit from inside like a lantern the size of a house.

02 — Still Gardens

Five chapters. Ninety minutes. One quiet mind.

Each chapter is a walk, not a lecture. You arrive at the gate, climb the steps, sit with the lantern, and leave with one thing worth keeping.

Note 01 — GravelRaked before first light

The garden is combed into lines that hold the moon. Footsteps are asked to wait until the pattern has finished its work.

Note 02 — SceneryThe mountain you never own

A gap in the hedge is measured to the inch, so a far ridge can sit inside the garden like a loan that is never called in.

Note 03 — RainWeather as a second architect

Night rain darkens the stone to ink and lifts the smell of cedar through the court. The walk is slower, and that is the point.

03 — Sacred Craft

Borrowed scenery, charred cypress, lantern light.

Shakkei: composing with a mountain you will never own. Yakisugi: burning a board black so the weather will let it live. How a single ember decides the scale of everything around it.

ShakkeiBorrowed scenery

Compose with what lies beyond the wall. The ridge, the moon, the far temple roof: none of it is yours, all of it is in the frame.

YakisugiCharred cypress

Burn the surface of the board until it turns to black glass. Fire, given first, is the weather the wood no longer fears.

AndonLantern light

Paper stretched over a cedar rib turns one ember into a room. Scale begins where the light decides to stop.

KairoThe covered walk

A roofed corridor that bends before it shows you the hall. Arrival is a sequence, never a single step.

04 — Afterlight

The gate does not close behind you.

Take the walk whenever the noise gets loud — it is always the same path, and never the same light.

The temple keeps its lamps lit long after the city has gone quiet. Whoever climbs at night carries a little of that stillness back down.
